Sydney has a new gateway to the world. Western Sydney International (or Nancy-Bird Walton Airport) is now open at Badgerys Creek, around 50 kilometres west of the CBD. Designed to take pressure off Sydney Airport while bringing travel closer to the city’s growing western suburbs, it’s a major new chapter for Australia’s biggest city.
Unlike Kingsford Smith, the new airport operates around the clock, without a curfew. That gives airlines greater flexibility to schedule overnight flights and opens up more opportunities for international connections.
The airport welcomed its first passenger flights on 25 October 2026, with Jetstar operating the inaugural service to the Gold Coast. International services include Air New Zealand flights to Auckland and Singapore Airlines’ upcoming daily connection to Singapore. Join us on a photo tour around the terminal and the international Rayleigh Lounge.
Inside the WSI terminal
The terminal has a contemporary, open design, with plenty of natural light and a clean, modern aesthetic. Its layout has been designed to support both domestic and international operations, with passenger facilities that can grow as traffic increases.
You’ll find departures on the left, where the check-in hall predominantly consists of self-serve kiosks. There’s also a premium check-in booth nearby that we understand will be used by select full-service airlines.
